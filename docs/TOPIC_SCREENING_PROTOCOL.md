# Topic Screening Protocol

**Status:** Preliminary scoping review in progress  
**Protocol date:** 2026-11-22  
**Purpose:** Identify a feasible computer-science research question with a plausible, evidence-based contribution and verifiable data access for a bachelor's-level project.

## Scope

The screen is limited to applied computer science topics that can be implemented and evaluated by one undergraduate researcher with ordinary computing resources. Initial areas are SMS spam/smishing, Persian/RTL interfaces and accessibility, software defect prediction, phishing URL detection, and vulnerability/software-supply-chain analysis. These are candidate areas, not preselected topics.

## Questions for each candidate

1. What is the narrow, testable research question and what result would answer it?
2. What are the closest primary studies, especially from 2024–2026, and how does the proposed question differ?
3. Can the data or test corpus be obtained from its official source, and are reuse and redistribution terms explicit?
4. Can the protocol be run with the available time, compute, and skills?
5. Are the outcome measures appropriate, and can the findings be stated without generalizing beyond the sample?

## Search plan

- **Search date:** 2026-11-21 (initial targeted search; update before any final topic selection).
- **Sources:** Crossref/DOI records, publisher or conference proceedings, arXiv for preprints clearly marked as such, official dataset repositories, standards bodies, and project repositories for license/provenance checks.
- **Date emphasis:** 2024–2026 for current overlap; older primary sources are retained when they define a benchmark, standard, or method.
- **Search method:** Search combinations of topic terms, task terms, data/validation terms, and evaluation terms. Follow references from directly relevant primary papers and search for close replications or competing datasets.
- **Record:** query, date, source, title, year, publication status, study/data relevance, license evidence, and reason for inclusion or exclusion.

## Eligibility rules

### Include

- Primary empirical studies, dataset papers, standards, and official repository documentation directly related to the candidate question.
- Studies with enough method detail to identify the data, task, and evaluation design.
- Sources that clarify dataset origin, labeling, license, access, or redistribution constraints.

### Exclude from novelty evidence

- Marketing pages, unsourced summaries, and non-primary commentary.
- Work that shares only broad keywords but studies a materially different task (retain as context where useful).
- Preprints represented as peer-reviewed publications; preprints may inform the scan but must be labeled.
- Dataset landing pages as proof of research quality; data source/license and study results are assessed separately.

## Candidate decision rubric

Score each item 0–2: **0** unresolved/weak; **1** plausible but needs verification; **2** supported by current evidence.

| Dimension | What a score of 2 requires |
|---|---|
| Research gap | Specific distinction from the closest recent primary studies; no broad novelty claim. |
| Data rights and access | Official source is reachable and reuse terms for the actual data are clear. |
| Feasibility | One student can complete collection/preparation, analysis, and reporting with available resources. |
| Evaluation validity | Protocol, baselines, metrics, and uncertainty/error analysis suit the question. |
| Reproducibility | Inputs, transformations, split rules, software versions, and outputs can be documented. |

A candidate is **not selected** if data rights/access score 0, or if research gap scores 0. Total scores are prioritization aids, not evidence of novelty or publication likelihood.

### Focused candidate refinement (2026-11-22)

Further primary-source checks found a concrete, narrower case and sharpened the project boundary:

- The pnpm issue tracker documents a two-document `pnpm-lock.yaml` emitted by pnpm 12 while both documents still declare lockfile version `9.0`. The report says single-document consumers can fail or read only the environment document and report zero dependencies; it describes Dependabot alerts auto-closing in the reporting repository. This is a maintainer issue report, not an independently reproduced result in this project. [pnpm issue #13805](https://github.com/pnpm/pnpm/issues/13805)
- The official pnpm page currently documents the lockfile format and describes the new v13 format as planned/experimental; this is a moving implementation detail, so any future experiment must pin exact package-manager versions and preserve generated fixture hashes. [pnpm lockfile settings](https://github.com/pnpm/pnpm/blob/main/pnpm/docs/settings/store.md)
- Gamage et al.'s *The Design Space of Lockfiles Across Package Managers* is listed by arXiv as published in *Empirical Software Engineering* (2026). It studies lockfile designs across seven managers and developer experience, rather than scanner behavior on pnpm's multi-document case. [arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.04834)

**Candidate question for a practical audit:** *For a minimal dependency graph, which selected open-source inventory/scanning tools recover the expected package coordinates from a pnpm single-document lockfile and a pnpm 12 two-document lockfile, and which failures are silent versus explicit?*

**Pre-registered-style plan (proposal only):**

1. **Scope:** npm ecosystem only; compare one pinned single-document control with one pinned pnpm 12 two-document fixture. Use the public reproduction shape from the pnpm issue (`is-odd@3.0.1` plus its dependency) only after confirming the exact generated graph. Do not evaluate exploit behavior or claim production-wide vulnerability accuracy.
2. **Ground truth:** package coordinates from the package-manager-resolved fixture, independently checked against the root manifest and lockfile graph. Pin the package-manager and scanner versions and save input/output hashes. npm identifies the `is-odd` and `is-number` packages as MIT-licensed, but the exact resolved version and its license must be rechecked before producing a fixed fixture. Do not redistribute package archives. [is-odd record](https://www.npmjs.com/package/is-odd) · [is-number record](https://www.npmjs.com/package/is-number)
3. **Comparison:** at least two open-source tools that expose machine-readable inventories; include parse-error, nonzero exit, empty-but-successful, partial, and exact-match outcomes. Select tool/version pairs only after confirming their supported input and output formats.
4. **Null hypothesis:** each tool returns the same expected package-coordinate set for both semantically equivalent lockfile fixtures. **Alternative:** at least one tool returns a missing/extra coordinate, explicit parse failure, or silently empty/partial inventory for the two-document fixture.
5. **Measures:** package-coordinate precision/recall against the fixture ground truth, exact-set match, missing and extra package counts, tool exit status, and silent-incompleteness indicator. These measure inventory extraction only; do not report vulnerability precision/recall or F1 without a separate fixed advisory truth set.
6. **Interpretation limits:** two fixtures and a small tool sample support a bounded case study only. They do not estimate prevalence across all lockfiles, managers, scanners, or repositories. A simple guard may be assessed as an engineering mitigation, but its warning behavior must be reported separately from scanner correctness.

**Topic gate:** the case is reproducible and likely useful as a software-engineering portfolio artifact, but the core failure is already publicly reported and the broader parser-confusion/benchmark area has prior work. Do not claim novelty or journal suitability from this case study alone. Before implementation, one more focused search must compare this exact pnpm scenario with the 2024 SBOM benchmark, current scanner tests/issues, and any newly published empirical study. No code, fixtures, packages, or vulnerability data have been downloaded or created.

### Candidate SMS security extension: message-length-bounded injection (2026-11-22)

**Discovery log:** On 2026-11-22, three targeted OpenAlex Works searches (`SMS prompt injection`, `SMS phishing prompt injection`, and `mobile messaging prompt injection length budget`) returned zero records each. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.15611/2025.07.4.02) · [author-uploaded text](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/403935032_Prompt_Injection_Attacks_on_LLM-Based_Spam_Filters)

The generic version of the SMS prompt-injection idea overlaps with Borkowski's 2025 proof-of-concept on LLM email spam filters and Zhu and Liu's November 2026 phishing-email study, which evaluates direct, generic-robust, and injection-first procedures on clean phishing, injected phishing, and matched injected legitimate emails. Koide et al. also study prompt injection against multimodal phishing-site detectors. An IEEE S&P 2025 poster already evaluates small instruction-tuned models for SMS smishing under textual perturbations, although its accessible text does not explicitly report classifier-directed prompt injection. Generic prompt hardening, an injection-first SMS classifier, small-model SMS classification, or generic perturbation robustness must not be claimed as novel. [Borkowski, 2025](https://doi.org/10.15611/2025.07.4.02) · [Zhu & Liu, 2026](https://publications.scrs.in/chapter/978-81-975670-4-9/3) · [Koide et al., 2026 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05484) · [IEEE S&P 2025 poster](https://sp2025.ieee-security.org/downloads/posters/sp25posters-final23.pdf)

**Narrower provisional candidate:** measure the label-flip curve as classifier-directed text is added within canonical GSM-7/UCS-2 single-message and concatenated-message segment budgets. Compare the published email-oriented inference conditions, including injection-first if faithfully reproducible, and test matched injected legitimate controls. GSM-7 and UCS-2 capacities differ under 3GPP SMS encoding; count encoded units and segment boundaries rather than ordinary characters alone. This could test transfer of email defenses to the short-message channel, but the novelty of the channel-budget angle is not established. [3GPP TS 23.040](https://www.etsi.org/deliver/etsi_ts/123000_123099/123040/17.02.00_60/ts_123040v170200p.pdf)

**New primary-source overlap found (2026-11-22):** *UniGuardian: A Unified Defense for Detecting Prompt Injection, Backdoor Attacks and Adversarial Attacks in Large Language Models* appeared in Findings of IJCNLP-AACL 2026. Its paper evaluates prompt-trigger detection on an SMS Spam test split (558 samples), alongside other datasets. This is direct evidence that SMS spam has already been used in prompt-injection/trigger-defense evaluation; it weakens any broad SMS-domain novelty claim. The paper does not, from the inspected methods, establish that SMS-encoded length budgets or segment boundaries were studied. [ACL Anthology paper](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-aacl.119/)

An August 2026 author-uploaded preprint also directly compares traditional TF-IDF and instruction-following classifiers under prompt injection on the SMS Spam Collection, using injected spam and ham examples. It is explicitly marked as a preprint and should be treated as non-peer-reviewed evidence, but it further narrows the possible contribution. The remaining length-budget idea is at most a candidate gap pending full related-work screening, not a novelty claim. [Preprint record and DOI](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21939849)
